## Architecture decisions

The live site already has all 16 requested brand hubs, a large brand/service matrix, and substantial Mercedes and Porsche model/system/problem libraries. Begin with **zero unconditional new URLs**. Select an existing commercial owner, improve its scope and links, and only separate an additional intent after checking its query evidence, content differences and existing URLs. A system's technical legitimacy does not establish search demand or prove DIGI-TEC's repair capability.

Use four distinct roles:

- Brand hub: workshop, garage, specialist, service-centre and broad repair intent for that marque in Dubai. Near-me and Al Quoz modifiers normally belong here, with accurate location and directions.
- Service owner: the repair/diagnostic decision and booking for a specific system. It can explain related symptoms, repair versus replacement, quote inputs, supported fitment and workshop evidence.
- Problem or system guide: an informational explanation useful before repair, with a contextual link to the service owner. Avoid repeating the service page's sales proposition and heading intent.
- Model guide: only material platform differences, mapped to existing service owners. A model name is insufficient reason for a new commercial page or a complete model-by-service matrix.

The [Porsche hub](https://digitecme.com/brands/porsche-service-dubai) already presents model, system and symptom routes. Its 911 navigation and the blog index both lead to [the same 911 guide](https://digitecme.com/blog/porsche-911-service-dubai-guide), so those routes alone are **not** evidence of two competing pages. Preserve the existing URL unless there is a demonstrated migration need.

## Symptom consolidation rules

This is a taxonomy design, not vehicle diagnosis. A symptom can have several causes, so route the reader to a diagnostic process and do not promise a named component needs replacement.

## Internal-link observations and recommended relationships

The existing Mercedes and Porsche hubs already link to services and problem/model guides. Improve links where they advance a reader's next decision, rather than adding another directory level. Each important service should have a link from its brand hub and relevant generic service hub; each problem/model guide should include one clear contextual route to the appropriate commercial owner. Reverse service-to-guide links should answer real pre-booking questions.

Specific sample: [AC-not-cold guide](https://digitecme.com/blog/car-ac-not-cold-dubai-causes) shows unrelated Mercedes valuation and oil-change recommendations in its continuation block, while the [older AC article](https://digitecme.com/blog/car-ac-repair-dubai) has a direct related-AC-service block. If the causes guide becomes the informational survivor, preserve that useful service link and select genuinely related content. Footer links alone do not communicate the symptom-to-service relationship as clearly.

Do not link every brand to every service/system/model simply to increase link count. Keep entity taxonomy intelligible: Mercedes–Maybach and Land Rover–Range Rover–Defender need clear cross-links and separate genuine owner intent, not parallel near-duplicate pages. Technical tooling pages should explain supported work and serve the repair decision, not promise dealership authorization, all-module access or universal online programming.

## Google Search policy and implementation constraints

- FAQ content remains useful for customers, but do not forecast FAQ rich-result gains. Google's [official updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) state that FAQ rich results stopped appearing from 7 May 2026, and the feature documentation was removed on 15 June 2026.
- [Canonical guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls) applies to duplicate or very similar pages. A canonical is a signal, not a guarantee. For a genuine consolidation later, migrate useful content, redirect retired duplicate URLs to the relevant survivor, align internal links and sitemap; do not canonicalize different helpful information to the hub merely because they mention the same brand.
- [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) identify doorway and scaled-content abuse. This does **not** prove a penalty here. It supports rejecting near-me/city permutations and largely interchangeable brand-service pages produced primarily to capture similar queries.
- [People-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) supports real workshop evidence and originality; it explicitly rejects a supposed preferred word count. Thinness means inadequate unique usefulness, not simply fewer than a numerical word threshold.
- [Google's link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) supports crawlable contextual links and at least one internal link to every important page. There is no magic link-count target.

No redirects, canonical changes, page creation, metadata/content edits, internal-link edits or publishing were performed. Proposed merges and content scope remain recommendations for a later approved implementation.
