    How We Diagnose a Mercedes Before Recommending Repairs

    A wrong diagnosis is the most expensive thing that can happen to a Mercedes. The same warning can come from four different parts, so we follow a predetermined sequence and only quote when we can identify the part that actually failed.

    1. 01

      What you tell us, and what the car tells us

      We start with the model, year, mileage, and exactly what you noticed, because when a symptom comes up regularly, it may matter more than the fault code. Then we check every module, not only the engine ECU, and confirm from the VIN which functions we can perform on your vehicle.

    2. 02

      Live data, component testing and a road test

      Fault codes are symptoms, not diagnoses. We review live data while the system is operating, run guided tests, and inspect the part before determining it is faulty. For drivability problems, we take it for a road test with logging on so the data is recorded, not just guessed.

    3. 03

      Root cause confirmed, then an itemized quote

      Both the failed part and the part that interacts with it can look similar in the scan results. We separate the two first, which explains why we may replace just one level sensor rather than four air struts. The quotations list parts and labor charges per item, and nothing starts until we get your approval.

    4. 04

      Repair, re-scan and verification

      After completion, we re-scan the system, reset service and adaptation values as needed for the job, and road-test against the original complaint. You get the car back with a short report on what we found, what we replaced, and what we'll watch.

    Common Faults We Diagnose at Our Mercedes Workshop in Dubai

    These are the symptoms Mercedes owners describe to us most often, and what each one usually turns out to be. These issues alone aren’t diagnostic, but they show you where to start your diagnostic process.

    Engine light or a warning you have never seen before

    This light indicates that one of the modules recorded a value outside its normal range, which could be a faulty sensor connection or a problem that needs immediate attention. The code pinpoints the issue; the live data confirms it. If the light is flashing and not steady, park and contact us immediately.

    Car is not starting, or key is not recognized

    Low voltage from a weak main or auxiliary battery can prevent communication between modules. This is why other warnings from completely different systems appear on the dashboard at once. We test voltage and charging before replacing anything.

    Gearbox jerks, shifts late, or shudders around 50 km/h

    Classic symptoms of 7G and 9G transmission under light load. Fluid condition, adaptation values, torque converter lockup, and the valve body look identical from the driver's seat, but they're repaired very differently. A road test with real data is the key here.

    Car sits low overnight, or an AIRMATIC warning appears

    This is often the first sign of an air suspension problem, and it's a cheaper issue to address. If one corner is lower, it may indicate a leaking strut, a poor valve block holding pressure, or a worn compressor. Left alone, the compressor will wear itself out while trying to compensate for the leak.

    AC is cold on the move but warm at every traffic light

    The complaint we hear most from May to September. It points to condenser airflow, a failing cooling fan, compressor control, or a charge that has quietly dropped, and it always worsens as summer goes on. An AC fault that only shows at idle is an early warning.

    Temperature gauge climbs in traffic

    Plastic thermostat housings, expansion tanks, and water pumps harden and crack under constant heat cycling here, and a fan that has stopped running only shows itself when the car is stationary. Overheating is one of the few faults worth pulling over for rather than driving home on.

    Auxiliary battery or 48V warning on the dash

    The most common Mercedes message in the UAE. Heat roughly halves auxiliary battery life compared with Europe, and a weak one can throw several unrelated warnings at once. On most models, you have to register the new battery so the charging system treats it correctly.

    Burning smell through the vents with nothing on the floor

    Oil reaching a hot exhaust component burns off before it ever reaches the ground, so the smell arrives months before the stain does. Likely sources include oil cooler seals on V6 platforms, turbo oil feed lines on biturbo V8s, or a valve cover gasket.

    Mercedes Service A and Service B in Dubai

    Mercedes doesn’t calculate the service interval based on mileage alone. ASSYST counts down based on how the car is actually driven, which is why even two very similar cars may require service at different intervals.
    Your car switches between two levels, and the level determines the scope of the visit.

    Mercedes Service A

    The lighter visit, usually first at around a year or 15,000 km. It includes synthetic approved oil and filter, checking and setting all fluid levels, tire condition and pressure, brakes, and resetting the maintenance indicator.

    Mercedes Service B

    All items covered in Service A, plus: combined cabin filter replacement, brake fluid change, and a wider inspection of the parts an A service visit only checks. Usually comes at the two-year mark, after which the car alternates A, B, A, B.
    No tier covers air filters, spark plugs, coolant, or gearbox fluid, but each has its own service codes, which is why one B service might cost more than the last.
    Send us your VIN and mileage, and we will tell you which tier you are on and what your car is actually asking for.
